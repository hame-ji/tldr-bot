Title: Replacing "Grill-Me" with "Grill With Docs" for AI-Assisted Coding

TL;DR: Transitioning to a "grill with docs" LLM prompt improves AI coding sessions by maintaining a dynamic, domain-specific glossary that ensures consistent terminology across generated code.

Key points:
- The standard "grill-me" prompting skill becomes inefficient for codebases because the AI forgets domain jargon and requires repetitive explanations.
- "Grill with docs" incorporates the "ubiquitous language" concept from Domain-Driven Design by maintaining a central `context.md` file.
- The AI actively challenges fuzzy language, refines definitions, and documents architectural decisions as the developer works.
- This rigorous focus on terminology directly dictates the naming conventions of all generated variables, files, and database entities.
- The standard "grill-me" prompt remains useful for non-codebase tasks, such as brainstorming or general writing.

Why it matters:
- Relying on a central terminology document produces more concise AI responses, saves tokens, and ensures the codebase accurately reflects domain expert language.

Evidence:
- The underlying methodology is based on the "ubiquitous language" concept from Eric Evans' Domain-Driven Design.
- User feedback indicated that within four to five sessions, the AI reliably utilized the shared context without needing definitions repeated.

Caveat:
- The heavy initial focus on establishing precise terminology can resemble "bikeshedding" and temporarily delay actual code implementation.
